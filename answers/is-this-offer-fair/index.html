What makes an offer fair. Five things to weigh.

  • Reach, not followers. Use what your last few pieces got by the same point after release. That is what a brand is buying.
  • The placement. A short mention, a full integration and a dedicated piece are different jobs at different prices.
  • Usage rights. If they want to run your video as an ad or keep using it, that is a licence on top. See who owns your video.
  • Exclusivity. Not working with their rivals for a time costs you deals. Price it.
  • When you get paid. Agree the payment date in writing. A fair price paid months late is not a fair deal.

Common questions. Short answers.

Is this sponsorship offer fair for my size?

Compare the offer with a fair range worked out from what your recent work actually reached, at a price per thousand people reached for creators your size. Below the range, counter at its middle. Inside it, the middle is a fair ask. Above it, take it and get the terms in writing. Orbe's free Fair offer check does this in your browser.

Should I price from my followers or my views?

From your views, listens or opens. Followers say how many people could see a post; recent views say how many did. Brands buy people reached, so a price built on what your last few pieces actually got is the one you can defend.

What if the brand says the offer is their whole budget?

It may be. You can still say what the work is worth, offer less work for their budget (a shorter mention instead of a full integration), or ask for something else of value, such as a longer deal or a second placement. Saying no to an underpriced deal is also a fair answer.

Do usage rights and exclusivity change the price?

Yes. A fair range covers the post itself. If the brand wants to run your video as an ad, use it on its own channels, or stop you working with its rivals for a time, that is extra and should be priced on top.
