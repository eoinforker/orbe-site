The words in the offer. In plain English.

Percentage or shares
Equity is offered as a slice of the company or as a number of shares. A number of shares means little until you know the total, so ask what percentage of the fully diluted shares it is.
Valuation
What the company says it is worth, usually set at its last funding round. It is not a price anyone will pay you for your slice.
Shares or options
Options are a right to buy shares later at a set exercise price. You may have to pay that price, and tax can fall due along the way.
Vesting and the cliff
Equity becomes yours over a period. Before the cliff, none of it is yours; if the deal ends early, unvested equity usually goes back.
Dilution
Each new funding round usually issues new shares, so your percentage shrinks even though your share count does not.
Preferences
Investors often hold preferred shares that are paid back first when a company is sold. In a modest sale, ordinary shares and options can get little or nothing.

Common questions. Short answers.

Should I take equity instead of cash for a brand deal?

Usually not instead; sometimes as well. Get a fair cash price for the work first and treat any equity as a bonus that may never pay. Most startup equity is worth nothing, and what does pay can take years. This is general information, not financial, legal or tax advice: speak to an adviser before you sign.

How do I work out what startup equity is worth?

You cannot know. You can only illustrate it: the paper value today (your percentage times the company's valuation, or your shares times the price per share), shrunk by future funding rounds, under a few outcomes, including the company failing and the equity being worth nothing. Orbe's equity mode shows this with every assumption on the page.

What does a vesting cliff mean?

Vesting is how the equity becomes yours over time. A cliff is a starting period during which nothing vests. If the partnership ends before the cliff, you get none of it; at the cliff, a first slice vests, then the rest month by month.

What is dilution?

When a company raises money it usually issues new shares, so each existing holder owns a smaller slice. Your number of shares stays the same, but your percentage falls with every round.

What should I ask before accepting equity?

What percentage of the fully diluted shares is this? Is it shares or options, and at what exercise price? What did the last round price a share at? Are there investor preferences ahead of mine? What happens to unvested equity if the deal ends early? And what tax will I owe, and when?
