Orbe wordmark, round 5Final and runner-up

The lap

The O of Orbe is one stroke that goes round twice: once as the letter, the world, then a second, finer lap inside it that comes to rest on a small ball just short of where it began. It is an orb and its orbit in a single movement of the pen, and every lap is measured against your own last one, which is what Orbe does for creators.

Construction

Drawn on a 1000 unit em, in the spirit of Timeless Serif so it sits with the site's type. The lap is the O's own counter, scaled to 60 percent: the letter holds a smaller copy of itself, measured against its own size.

The O

Cap height 700 with 12 of overshoot, near round (680 by 724) like Timeless. Vertical stress: 102 at the sides, 30 at top and bottom, a squarer counter than the outline.

The lap

An ellipse inside the counter, tangent to it at 2 o'clock and set 10 units into the wall. It leaves the wall with a crisp acute crotch, the way the b's shoulder leaves its stem, travels 306 degrees anticlockwise (the direction an O is written), and swells to 37 on its upright sides.

The ball

A 24 unit ball ends the lap just short of where it began: the landing. It rhymes with the r's 45 unit ball terminal, so the detail belongs to the family rather than sitting on top of it.

r, b, e

Wedge flags on r and b, an arched r shoulder into a pendant ball, a bracketed slab foot, a b with an angled heel, and an e with a level bar at 53 percent. Spacing: O to r 40, r to b 42, b to e 48.

Runner-up: Axis

The O's stress is tilted 23.4 degrees, the Earth's axial tilt, which is also where old style type puts the pen. On reveal the counter would turn like a world and settle on its axis. It is calm, it reads perfectly at every size, and it is the safer choice.

Why it lost

At a glance it is a well-drawn O and nothing more. The critique of rounds 4 and 5 was "typeset Orbe with a tweaked O at most", and Axis is exactly that, only better made. Its idea needs a caption.

Why the lap won

You can see the idea without being told: one stroke, two laps, a landing. It is lettering, not a symbol; it reads as Orbe at 14px; and the reveal is the idea itself, the pen going round twice.

Risks we checked

An open lap read as a c inside an O, which made the O look like a copyright sign, so the lap now closes to 306 degrees. A brushed version read as an enso, so the drawing is strictly geometric.

How we got here

Six directions first, judged at 18, 48 and 200px on paper and ink beside the Smith & Diction sheet, then the two survivors refined in eleven passes.

Favicon

The O alone, recut heavier for 16 to 32px (thicker lap and ball) on an ink tile, so it holds on light and dark browser tabs.
