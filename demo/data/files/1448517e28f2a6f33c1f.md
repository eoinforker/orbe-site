# The Orbe Rating: the maths

Model version **2026.10**. This page is generated from the same data the rating engine runs on (`src/rating/kinds.ts`), so it cannot disagree with it. Regenerate it with `node --experimental-strip-types --no-warnings src/rating/maths.ts --write`.

## What the number means

The Orbe Rating is a performance measure, not a talent score. Zero is the typical creator of your kind and size. A positive rating means your recent work is doing better than creators in your size band; a negative one means it is doing worse. A small creator can be Great.

Independently calculated by Orbe. It is not a YouTube metric, and it is not provided or endorsed by YouTube or Google. The rating, its bands, sub-scores, per-work ratings, personal bests and benchmarks are all calculated by Orbe from the numbers described below. On YouTube those numbers are public statistics (views, likes, comments, subscribers) read through the YouTube Data API; numbers a creator connects through YouTube Analytics are shown only to that creator and to the people they choose, one at a time, and are not inputs to the rating.

| Band | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| Great | +2.00 or more |
| Good | +0.80 or more |
| Average | above -0.80 |
| Subpar | above -2.00 |
| Poor | anything lower |

## How it is built

1. **Matured numbers.** Most of a work's numbers arrive in its first days, so a young work's counts are scaled up to what they should be once it has matured: a count is divided by the share expected by its age, 1 - e^(-age / tau). We never assume less than 5% has arrived, so a work minutes old is not multiplied a thousandfold. Works too young to judge are left out of your rating, though they get their own rating straight away.
2. **Medians, not means.** A typical work is the median work, so one viral hit or one flop moves it by at most one place. Where a ratio is pooled across works (likes over views, say), no single work may weigh more than a few times the median work.
3. **Parts.** Each part of the rating measures one thing: reach, momentum, hit rate, engagement, cadence, and the parts particular to your kind, such as paid conversion for a newsletter.
4. **Scores against your size.** Each part is compared with a prior for your kind, format and size band: a typical value and a spread. Counts and ratios spread multiplicatively, so we compare on a log scale: z = ln(yours / typical) / spread. Shares that sit between 0 and 1, like hit rate, compare on a straight scale: z = (yours - typical) / spread.
5. **No runaway parts.** Every z is capped at plus or minus 3, so no one part can carry the rating.
6. **Small samples.** With few works, chance looms large, so each part's z is multiplied by the square root of (works / works needed for full weight) until there are enough. Cadence is not shrunk: publishing rarely is itself the signal.
7. **Sub-scores.** Each part is shown as a percentile from 0 to 100: the share of creators your size you beat on it, if creators spread as the prior says. 50 is typical.
8. **The rating.** Rating = 2 x (the weighted average of the parts' z-scores), minus the season's offset. It runs from -6 to +6; most creators sit between -2 and +2.
9. **Missing numbers.** Some parts need numbers only a connected account can give (opens, completion). When a part cannot be measured it is set aside and the others are reweighted in proportion. If less than half the weight can be measured, we say so rather than guess.
10. **Not enough work yet.** Below the minimum number of matured works, there is no rating: we tell you how many more you need.

## Priors, versions and seasons

Every prior below is **provisional** until it says otherwise: a stated guess, not a measurement. Each cell (kind, format, size band, part) is replaced by a measured median and spread once at least 30 creators in that cell have consented to be counted. Measured spreads use the median absolute deviation, and are never allowed below 25% of the provisional spread.

Each season the scale is recentred on that season's consented creators, so it cannot slowly inflate: every part's typical value moves to the cohort's median, and an offset is set so the cohort's median rating is exactly zero, per size band where the band has enough creators and per kind otherwise.

Every rating records the model version that produced it. When the maths changes, the version changes, old ratings keep the version they were made with, and the change is noted at the end of this page.

## YouTube channel (`youtube`)

Size bands, by subscribers: Under 10k, 10k to 100k, 100k to 250k, 250k to 1M, 1M to 5M, Over 5M.

Formats are rated apart: Shorts are works of 1 to 180 seconds; everything else is long-form. You are rated on the format you make most of, against priors for that format.

Metrics each work carries:

| Metric | Key | How it matures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Views | `views` | about 63% in 9 days, 86% in 18 days |
| Likes | `likes` | about 63% in 9 days, 86% in 18 days |
| Comments | `comments` | about 63% in 9 days, 86% in 18 days |

A work counts towards your rating once it is 2 days old. We need 3 such works to rate you at all, and use your newest 50. With fewer than 10, each part is pulled towards typical.

### Weights

| Part | Weight | What it measures | Provisional prior: median by size band, spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reach | 30% | the median of views / subscribers across your works | 0.3 / 0.2 / 0.15 / 0.1 / 0.065 / 0.04, spread 0.9 (log) |
| Momentum | 25% | the median views of your newest 10 works against the median of the 20 before them (a third and the rest, for shorter histories) | 1, spread 0.45 (log) |
| Hit rate | 20% | the share of your works with views at 2 times your own median or better | 0.12, spread 0.08 |
| Engagement | 15% | (likes + comments) / views, pooled across your works, with no work weighing more than 3 times the median work | 0.035, spread 0.6 (log) |
| Cadence | 10% | works a month over the last 182 days (or the span we can see, if shorter), docked when the gaps between works are uneven | 4, spread 0.8 (log) |

Shorts use their own priors. Reach: 0.5 / 0.35 / 0.25 / 0.15 / 0.1 / 0.06, spread 1 (log). Engagement: 0.04, spread 0.6 (log). Cadence: 8, spread 0.8 (log).

### One work

A single upload is rated on its views as a multiple of your own median for its format (spread 0.7), with 25% on (likes + comments) / views against your own (spread 0.5).

Records we celebrate: most views on an upload; most likes on an upload; most comments on an upload; best first hour; best first day.

## Personal bests

A personal best is the moment one of your works beats every other work you have made on a record metric. Best starts compare works only at the same age, and only against works we saw that young. We wait until you have at least three works with the metric, so your first upload is not a record by default.
