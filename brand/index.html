[01]The recommendation

Blue leads. Pink is the dawn.

The brand is a sky blue field with ink type, a cobalt action and an ember spark, set on warm paper. Pink stays in the paintings and on the odd sticker. It is never a field beside cobalt.

Pink-led: Peony, Claret, Ink, Paper, Ember

Bold and warm, but it lands next to TypeSafe and, with any blue beside it, next to Idyll. Peony cannot carry white text, its action has to be ink, and it sits too close to a sale tag or a loss in money screens.

For creators

A bright sky field and a creature to play with feels like a favourite tool, not a bank. Cobalt is a confident, friendly button. Their own work, painted or posted, is the warm thing on the page.

For backers

Blue is the colour people already trust with money, so the fun reads as confidence, not noise. Ink and paper carry every figure; cobalt carries every action. It looks like a company that will still be careful at scale.

Why it is ours

fal pairs its blue with violet, yellow and lime; Idyll pairs magenta with electric blue. Orbe pairs fal's sky with cobalt, an ember spark, warm paper and painted landscapes. Nobody else has that set.

Always

  • One field per screen. Sky is the field; paper is where people read and where products live.
  • Ink for type on sky, paper and ember. Cobalt for the one action, links and the ball on the card.
  • Ember as a spark: bars, creatures, a crayon loop. On a light field it carries an ink edge whenever it must read as a shape.
  • Functional colours only for states in the product, always with words beside them.

Never

  • Never colour a rating red or green. A rating measures; it does not judge.
  • Never put paper or white text on sky or peony, or body text in cobalt on sky.
  • Never set peony or magenta beside cobalt as two fields. That is someone else's brand.
  • Never use gradients, glows or a second field colour on one screen. No seals, badges or ticks.
  • Never recolour a creator's work to match the brand.

[04]Texture and image

Pixels for the machine. Paint for the people.

The pixel side

fal's energy and TypeSafe's confidence. Use it on the brand field, around the rating and the product, and wherever Orbe is counting. Square cells on a grid, never blurred, never scaled by a fraction.

The painted side

The idyll's warmth. Use it where a person and their work are the subject: the hero's ground, the archive, the after-post email, the deck. Brushed by hand in code, layered, dry at the edges, crayon on top.

Framing the creator's own work

  • Their work is shown as they made it: cropped to fill, never filtered, dithered, tinted or painted over.
  • It sits in corner brackets, a folder or the card's window, with a small dark tooltip naming it: artefact 61 [episode 61: bread]
  • Orbe's own paintings stand in only where there is no work to show, and are labelled as made up.

Motion

  • Motion explains. A rating counts up once from zero, a folder lifts to show a card, the wordmark is cut once in the nav.
  • Play overshoots, money settles. Creatures, pills and dealt cards may spring; figures, fees and payouts never bounce.
  • Pixels step, paint fades. Creatures step at 110ms a frame; brush layers fade in over 700ms, 120ms apart.
  • Reduced motion turns every movement into an instant change.
